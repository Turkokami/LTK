# CONTENT.md — editorial rules

## Who you are writing for

A licensed applicator, on a phone, between stops, possibly wearing gloves. They arrived from a
search results page with a specific question. Answer it in the first sixty words.

Never write homeowner-facing copy. Never give treatment advice to the public. Never say
"exterminator" in first-party voice — the trade says pest management professional, technician or
applicator. The one exception is the Arena championship brand.

## Page opening pattern

Every Academy, Lab and Wire page:

1. Eyebrow — hub, template, state code
2. H1 — the page's subject, sentence case
3. `<QuickAnswer>` — target query as H2, 40–60 word standalone answer, one hard fact,
   verification date, named reviewer
4. Then the long form

The answer must survive being lifted out of context. No "as described above", no "see the table
below", no pronouns pointing at surrounding copy.

## Sourcing

- Every regulatory figure carries a source URL and a verification date.
- Every regulatory page carries a named author and a named reviewer.
- When a figure is unverified, publish the honest gap. `LabelBlock` renders `null` as
  *"Not yet published — verifying with the state agency."* That is a better page than a confident
  wrong number, and a better page than no page at all.
- Never source a regulatory fact from a competitor's blog post. Agency or nothing.

## Voice

- Specific over clever. "50 states, verified quarterly" beats "comprehensive coverage."
- Active voice. Buttons say what happens: "Verify my licence," not "Submit."
- One name per action through the whole flow. "Join" produces "Joined."
- Delete: seamless, empower, unlock, revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changing, robust,
  leverage (as a verb), solutions (as a noun).
- Empty states are invitations. Errors say what happened and how to fix it, without apologising.
- Each element does one job. A label labels. An example demonstrates. Nothing does both.

## Forum content

The forum is the compounding asset and needs the lightest possible hand:

- Read access fully open so threads index. Only posting is gated.
- Verified badges on every post, drawn from the verification record, never self-reported.
- Moderate for accuracy on anything safety- or label-related. A wrong dilution rate in a thread is
  a real-world harm, not a content problem.
- Never delete a thread to tidy up. Thread URLs are permanent — answer engines penalise broken
  citations far more than they reward fresh ones.

## Sponsored content

- Labelled at the top, in the label system, not in small print at the bottom.
- Never inside `/lab/reviews/` or `/lab/compare/`. The independence of those routes is what makes
  the sponsorship inventory worth anything.
- `/about/sponsorship-policy/` states the wall publicly. Counterintuitively, publishing the wall
  is what lets you charge more.
